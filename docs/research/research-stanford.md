## 1. Stanford 2027-28 cycle facts

**Testing (policy page checked 2026-09-23; reinstatement announced June 2024, confirmed Aug 2025).** SAT or ACT scores are required for all first-year applicants beginning with the fall 2026 entry cycle, so Eduardo's cycle (fall 2028 entry) is squarely inside the requirement. Stanford superscores both tests ("enter your highest Evidence-Based Reading and Writing and Math scores"), has no preference between SAT and ACT, and accepts self-reported scores for review (official scores only if you enrol). Score choice is not restricted for the SAT, but if you sit any AP exam you must report all AP scores. Latest sitting accepted: SAT by end of October for REA (so the Oct 2, 2027 SAT is the last usable date; scores arrive ~2 weeks later, tight but permitted), ACT by end of September. English proficiency tests are not required for undergraduates and cannot substitute for SAT/ACT; English A Literature in the IB plus an English-medium school profile is sufficient. Sources: [Stanford testing page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/testing.html), [Stanford Report, June 2024](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2024/06/stanford-to-resume-standardized-test-requirement), [Stanford Daily, Aug 6 2025](https://stanforddaily.com/2025/08/06/stanford-to-continue-legacy-admissions-reinstate-standardized-test-requirements/), [International applicants page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/international/index.html).

**REA rules (decision-process page, checked 2026-09-23).** Deadline November 1 (October 15 if submitting an optional arts portfolio); decision mid-December; non-binding, reply by May 1. While applying REA you may not apply to any other private college under EA/REA/ED/Early Notification, nor to a public under a binding ED plan. Exceptions: non-binding public EA (e.g., UT Austin, Michigan, Georgia Tech), rolling admission, non-U.S. universities (so UK/Canada/Brazil applications are fine), military academies, and any non-binding early deadline required for a scholarship or special program (USC is the named example). A small percentage of REA applicants are deferred to RD; the rest are admitted or denied (denied applicants cannot reapply RD). RD deadline January 5; midyear transcript February 15. Financial-aid priority date November 15. Sources: [REA/RD page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/decision_process.html), [first-year overview](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/index.html).

**Admit rates (Common Data Set 2025-26 for Class of 2029).** 60,646 applied, 2,302 admitted, 3.8%. Stanford stopped publishing REA statistics after the Class of 2020 (9.5%, 745/7,822) and released no data at all for the Class of 2030 in Dec 2025/Mar 2026. Counselor consensus puts REA at roughly 7-9% and RD at 2-3%, but those are inferences, not Stanford numbers. International: the Class of 2029 profile lists 237 international citizens (14%) from 70 countries; CDS 2025-26 shows 13.1% nonresident aliens. Stanford does not publish an international admit rate; independent estimates cluster around 1-2%, and the pool is need-aware, so a Brazilian applying for aid competes in a narrower lane. Sources: [CDS 2025-26 summary](https://www.commondatasets.fyi/stanford), [Ivy Coach statistics](https://www.ivycoach.com/the-ivy-coach-blog/college-admissions/stanford-admissions-statistics/), [Stanford Facts Class of 2029](https://facts.stanford.edu/academics/undergraduate-education/class-2029-profile), [Oriel on REA data](https://orieladmissions.com/stanford-early-action-acceptance-rate/).

**Need-aware for internationals (international page, checked 2026-09-23).** "For international citizens who indicate on their application that they will be applying for financial aid, that request will be a factor in our admission evaluation." Stanford meets full demonstrated need for admitted internationals who applied for aid, but an international who says "no aid" at application cannot request aid later. Stanford is the only HYPSM school still need-aware for internationals (endowing need-blind is a stated ~$200M goal). Sources: [International applicants](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/international/index.html), [REA international aid requirements](https://financialaid.stanford.edu/undergrad/apply/requirements/rea_international.html), [Stanford Review analysis](https://stanfordreview.org/stanfords-equity-facade-unmasking-the-reality-behind-need-blind-admission-policies/).

**Interview (interview page, checked 2026-09-23).** Optional, alumni-run, offered purely by volunteer availability in the applicant's school area (67+ international locations; Brazil availability not listed explicitly, so treat as possible, not guaranteed). REA interviews happen in the first few weeks of November, ~40 minutes, in person or video, no parents present. Declining carries no penalty. Interview is only "considered" in the CDS. Source: [Interview page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/interview.html).

**Recommendations (forms page, checked 2026-09-23).** School Report plus counselor letter (must come from a school official), and "letters of recommendation from two academic teachers. We recommend requesting letters from grade 11 or 12 teachers in English, math, science, world language, or history/social studies." A grade-10 teacher is acceptable if the course was advanced. One optional additional recommender who "can provide new insights." For IB outside the U.S., "academic results and predictions are required if available"; the IBO statement adds that a school official must send predicted marks including TOK/EE bonus points. Transcripts and letters must be translated by school officials, not the student. Sources: [Forms page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/forms.html), [IBO Stanford statement](https://recognition.ibo.org/en-US/university-statements/?id=96bcfca1-f0a1-ed11-aad1-000d3a85c377&university=Stanford+University).

**Essays (apply page, 2026-27 cycle; the wording has been stable for several years).** Common App personal essay (650 words; prompts unchanged 2026-27). Stanford-specific: five short questions, 50 words each: (1) most significant challenge society faces; (2) how you spent your last two summers; (3) historical moment you wish you had witnessed; (4) elaborate on one extracurricular, job, or family responsibility; (5) list five things important to you. Three essays, 100-250 words each: (A) "Reflect on an idea or experience that makes you genuinely excited about learning" (intellectual vitality); (B) note to your future roommate; (C) what aspects of your life experiences, interests, and character would help you make a distinctive contribution. Stanford's page asks that "your genuine voice come through." Common App's AI policy allows brainstorming and grammar checks but not AI-generated content or ideas. Sources: [Application and essays](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/apply.html), [College Transitions 2026-27 prompts](https://www.collegetransitions.com/blog/stanford-supplemental-essay-prompts/), [Common App prompts 2026-27](https://www.commonapp.org/blog/announcing-2026-2027-common-app-essay-prompts/).

**How IB is read.** Stanford's holistic page: "The primary criterion for admission to Stanford is academic excellence, which means flawless or nearly flawless grades in rigorous courses." There is no IB cutoff. Rigor is judged relative to what the school offers; three HLs including Math AA HL and Physics HL is the strongest STEM signal the IB allows, and credit is granted only for HL scores, which is a proxy for how Stanford weights HL over SL. A predicted 43-45 with 7s in the HLs reads as "top of a rigorous program"; a 41-42 with a 5 in an HL is where readers start asking questions. Sources: [Holistic admission](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/overview/index.html), [Selection process](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/selection/index.html), IBO statement above.

**Intellectual vitality, in Stanford's words.** "Stanford students are brimming with curiosity, openness, and imagination... we want to hear about the ways you have expanded your perspective and sought new opportunities," and "the initiative with which you seek out opportunities and expand your perspective." It is not "smart"; it is evidence of self-directed learning beyond assignments, ideally visible in the transcript, the recs, the activities, and Essay A simultaneously.

**Apply to Eduardo.**
- Lock the plan as SAT-only (no ACT), superscore-aware: Nov 7 2026 first sitting, Mar 6 2027 retake, with May 1/Jun 5 2027 as backups and Oct 2 2027 as an emergency-only date. Target composite 1550+ (CDS middle 50%: 1520-1570; math 770-800).
- Decide the financial-aid question with the parent by 2027-06-30: "no aid" removes the need-aware handicap; "aid" preserves eligibility for life. This is the single largest strategic lever outside the file itself and belongs to the parent.
- Recommenders: one from Math AA HL or Physics HL, one from English A Lit or Economics HL (different dimensions). Counselor letter is mandatory; the counselor relationship starts now, not in August 2027.

## 2. What actually moves the needle at ~1% odds

**Evidence from the CDS (2025-26).** Stanford marks as "very important": rigor of secondary record, academic GPA, standardized test scores, application essay, recommendations, extracurricular activities, talent/ability, character/personal qualities (some readers of the form also list class rank). Interview, first-gen, legacy, geography, volunteer work, work experience are only "considered"; demonstrated interest is not considered. The practical reading: no component can be neglected, and the non-numeric ones (essays, recs, ECs, talent, character) carry the same weight as the numeric ones. Source: [CDS C7](https://www.commondatasets.fyi/stanford), [Cosmic College CDS analysis](https://www.cosmic.nyc/blog/stanford-common-data-set-2025-2026).

**Depth over breadth is Stanford's own language.** "An exceptional depth of experience in one or two activities may demonstrate your passion more than minimal participation in five or six clubs." Former dean Richard Shaw (2011, when alumni interviews were introduced) said wealthy applicants often came in "overly packaged" and that readers want honesty, not consultant presentations. The "spike" thesis from counselors is the same idea: a file whose transcript, recs, activities and essays all point at one credible story. Sources: [Selection page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/selection/index.html), [Inside Higher Ed 2011](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2011/05/13/stanford-adds-alumni-interviews), [Oriel on spikes](https://orieladmissions.com/college-application-spike-extracurricular-strategy/).

**Turning PeakScore into a credible spike.** Counselors and verification reporting agree on what makes a founder claim believable: (a) hard numbers with dates (registered users, monthly actives, schools signed, students served, revenue, retention), (b) third-party evidence (a partner school director's letter as the optional additional recommender, press in a named outlet, an award from a competition with published judging), (c) a trajectory (10th-grade hobby → 11th-grade product with paying schools → 12th-grade measurable outcomes), (d) an intellectual layer beyond selling: a study of whether PeakScore users' scores actually rose, ideally with a control group, which doubles as EE material and as the substance of Essay A. Admissions offices verify big claims by calling the school or organisation, so every number in the activities list must be reproducible from a document. Sources: [Spark Admissions on verification](https://www.sparkadmissions.com/blog/do-colleges-verify-your-extracurriculars/), [Education Next on student founders](https://www.educationnext.org/service-or-signaling-surge-of-youth-nonprofit-founders/), [Oriel activities list](https://orieladmissions.com/common-app-activities-list-how-to-write/).

## 4. Grades and predicted grades

**How U.S. readers use predictions.** Stanford requires "academic results and predictions if available" from IB schools abroad and asks the school official to send predicted marks including the TOK/EE bonus. Readers see three things at once: the DP1 year-end grades on the transcript, the predicted total on the School Report, and the counselor's narrative ranking Eduardo within the class. There is no cutoff; predictions are read alongside course choice and school context. Sources: [Forms page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/forms.html), [IBO Stanford statement](https://recognition.ibo.org/en-US/university-statements/?id=96bcfca1-f0a1-ed11-aad1-000d3a85c377&university=Stanford+University), [Lanterna US IB guide 2026](https://lanterna.com/resources/top-us-universities-ib-recognition-2026-guide).

**What 43 vs 45 implies.** Both clear "flawless or nearly flawless." The difference readers notice is where the lost points are: a 43 built from 7-7-7 in the HLs and a 6 in a SL is effectively a 45 for a STEM/econ applicant; a 43 with a 6 in Math AA HL or Physics HL is a question mark against the intended major. HL 7s in Math AA and Physics, plus a 7 in Econ HL, are the marks that matter; SL 6s are survivable.

**Why DP1 year-end grades are decisive.** Predictions for REA are set by teachers in Aug-Sep 2027 using DP1 finals, mocks, IAs and coursework (there is no IBO formula; each school decides). The only transcript grades Stanford will see at REA are DP1 grades and any earlier years. A midyear report (Feb 15, 2028) only matters if deferred. So May-June 2027 exams are, for admission purposes, the final exams. Source: [TutorsPlus on predicted grades](https://tutorsplus.com/ib-predicted-grades-what-are-they-and-why-are-they-important/), [MyRevisionAgent](https://myrevisionagent.com/guides/ib-predicted-grades).

**Do effort/consistency comments matter?** Yes, indirectly. The School Report asks the counselor to rate academic achievement, intellectual promise, quality of writing, initiative and to compare the student with others in the class; the teacher form asks about intellectual curiosity and classroom contribution. Teachers who write "consistent," "asks questions beyond the syllabus," "taught the class X" supply the evidence for intellectual vitality; a term of "coasting" comments on the internal report will leak into the recommendations even if the grades hold.

**Apply to Eduardo.**
- Grade targets: 7s in Math AA HL, Physics HL, Econ HL; 6-7 in Chem SL, English A SL, Portuguese A SL; TOK/EE bonus 2-3. Predicted 43+ minimum, 44-45 is the plan. Weekly Notion check on every IA deadline and every school test (the pre-learn steps already in the plan feed this).
- Chemistry SL test Oct 9, 2026 and every subsequent internal test count toward the teacher's prediction; treat each as an exam.
- In April 2027, ask each HL teacher, in person, what would justify a 7 prediction; record the answer.
- Ask the counselor in Aug 2027 to preview the predicted total before it is sent.

## 5. Risks: what sinks strong international applicants

1. **Generic or ghostwritten essays.** Stanford wants "your genuine voice"; readers of the five 50-word answers can spot a committee. AI-generated content violates Common App policy. A parent-lawyer's polish is a risk, not an asset; the adult role is questions, not sentences. Sources: [Stanford essays page](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/apply.html), [GradPilot on Common App AI rules](https://gradpilot.com/news/common-app-ai-policy-paradox).
2. **Unverifiable founder claims.** Big claims trigger verification calls to the school or organisation; inflated user counts are the classic failure. Every number must trace to a document.
3. **Over-scheduling / breadth.** Stanford's own words favour depth in one or two activities. Six new clubs in 2027 signal packaging, not vitality. Soccer stays because it is real and it is CAS; nothing else new unless it feeds the spike.
4. **Recs from teachers who cannot tell a story.** A short, formal, Brazilian-style letter ("excellent student, always punctual") is fatal at 1% odds. Choose teachers who have seen him do something unusual; give them anecdotes; explain the U.S. genre.
5. **Testing gaps.** Missing the Oct 2027 SAT window with a sub-1500 score leaves the file weak in a "very important" factor. Two spring sittings eliminate this.
6. **Need-aware surprise.** Ticking "applying for aid" without the parent having decided makes the pool harder for no reason; ticking "no" without a real plan is worse.
7. **DP1 grade dip.** Predictions are formed on DP1; a 5 in an HL cannot be repaired before REA.
8. **Mental health and burnout.** Founder + three HLs + SAT + soccer is a burnout profile. Sleep, one protected weekend day, and a parent who watches for it are part of the system, not outside it.
9. **What Stanford explicitly says it does not want or use.** No formula, no minimum GPA or test score, no required number of advanced courses, athletics never sufficient alone, demonstrated interest not considered, interview never required, arts portfolio optional. Don't spend effort on demonstrated interest or on a Stanford summer program "for the badge."

**Apply to Eduardo.**
- Rule for adults (parent, Claude): we may ask questions, check facts and flag clichés; we do not write sentences in his essays.
- Every activity-list number links to a file in the PeakScore evidence log.
- No new club after 2027-01-01 unless it is a competition tied to the spike.
- Two spring SAT sittings booked by 2027-02-19.
- Parent owns the aid decision (by 2027-06-30) and the CSS Profile.
- Notion tracks sleep/load weekly; a red week triggers dropping something.
