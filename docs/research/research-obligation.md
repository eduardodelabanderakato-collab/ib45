# Making study "almost obligatory": what the evidence says (for Eduardo, IB DP1, target 45/45)

Evidence labels: **RCT/field experiment** (strongest), **meta-analysis**, **observational**, **practitioner** (no controlled evidence). Effect sizes are given where the source reports them.

---

## 1. Commitment devices

**The headline study is now retracted.** Ariely & Wertenbroch (2002), the paper behind "self-imposed deadlines work," was retracted by *Psychological Science* on 3 Sept 2026 after Data Colada found altered grades in Study 1 and implausible data in Study 2. With the grades recomputed, students who *chose* their own spaced deadlines did **worse** (M = 84.9) than students *assigned* evenly spaced deadlines (M = 88.8), t(68) = 2.83, p = .006 ([Data Colada 139](https://datacolada.org/139); [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/09/03/procrastination-study-duke-dan-ariely-psychological-science-data-colada-tampering-retraction/)). The independent replication published in July 2026 found demand for precommitment but not effectiveness.

**What survives:** self-set soft deadlines with no consequence do little. Bisin & Hyndman (2020, field experiment, students): strong demand for self-imposed deadlines, "deadlines do not appear to increase task completion rates," and students set them badly because of partial naivety ([GEB](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899825619301757)). Burger, Charness & Lynham (2011): interim self-set deadlines had a *negative* effect on study completion ([JEBO](https://faculty.econ.ucsb.edu/~charness/papers/procrastination.pdf)).

**What works: commitments with a counterparty and a task-level target.**
- Bryan, Karlan & Nelson (2010) review: "soft" commitments (reputational/psychological cost) can work as well as "hard" ones (money), and demand for hard commitment is low ([Annual Review of Economics](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/10.1146/annurev.economics.102308.124324)).
- Giné, Karlan & Zinman (2010, RCT, CARES smoking deposit contract): only 11% took it up; among those offered, +3 pp quit at 6 months, persisting at 12 months ([AEJ Applied](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.2.4.213)). Real effect, low take-up, high forfeiture risk.
- Royer, Stehr & Sydnor (2015, RCT, Fortune-500 gym): a 4-week incentive doubled attendance but faded; adding a *self-funded commitment contract* produced changes still detectable a year later ([AEJ Applied](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.20130327)).
- Himmler, Jäckle & Weinschenk (2019, RCT, university students): a **non-binding signed agreement** to stay on track raised exam registration, participation and passing; a **pure reminder arm did nothing**; procrastinators gained most; 5-year follow-up: −9 pp dropout, graduation 0.35 semesters earlier ([AEJ Applied](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.20170288)).
- Clark, Gill, Prowse & Rush (2020, two RCTs, ~4,000 students): **task-based goals** ("complete the practice exams") raised completion and course grades; **performance-based goals** ("get grade X") had no effect ([REStat](https://direct.mit.edu/rest/article/102/4/648/96785)).

**Stakes for teenagers.** Levitt, List, Neckermann & Sadoff (2016, RCTs, Chicago schools): incentives only work when delivered **immediately** ("all motivating power vanishes" with delay); **loss framing** beat gain framing (a $3 trophy framed as a loss: +0.18–0.25 SD for younger students); non-financial rewards work less well for older students ([AEJ Policy](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fpol.20130358)). Fryer (2011, RCTs, 200+ schools): paying for **inputs** (books read, homework) moved behaviour; paying for **outputs** (test scores) did nothing ([QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/126/4/1755/1924375)).

**What backfires.** (a) *Public identity pledges*: Gollwitzer et al. (2009) found that identity-related intentions noticed by others were acted on **less**, especially among highly committed people ([Psych Sci](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02336.x)). (b) *Peer-visible effort*: Bursztyn & Jensen (2015, RCT in LA high schools) — SAT-prep sign-up fell from ~80% (private) to 53% when classmates would know ([QJE](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/130/3/1329/1935006)). (c) Grade-based stakes (Clark; Fryer). (d) Deadlines he sets alone with no one watching (Bisin & Hyndman).

**Apply to Eduardo**
- Commit to *inputs* (blocks done, papers completed), never to grades. Sign a written, non-binding-but-witnessed agreement (Himmler): this is the cheapest device with the best education-specific evidence.
- Stakes must be immediate, loss-framed and private: a small weekly pot he forfeits per missed block, settled the same day. Not a classmate-visible pledge, not "I'm going to be a 45 student" announced to friends.
- Do not rely on deadlines he sets for himself with no counterparty; the retraction removed the only evidence they ever had.

## 2. Social accountability

**The Matthews "Dominican" study is weak evidence.** 267 recruited, only 149 completed; self-rated achievement; five groups of ~30; released as a press release and conference presentation, not a peer-reviewed journal article; Group 5 (written goals + action commitments + weekly progress report to a friend) reported ~76% success vs 43% for unwritten goals ([Dominican Scholar](https://scholar.dominican.edu/psychology-faculty-conference-presentations/3/)). Direction plausible, numbers not trustworthy.

**Stronger evidence that reporting to someone raises follow-through**
- Kast, Meier & Pomeranz (RCT, 2,687 Chilean micro-entrepreneurs): weekly self-help peer groups where members announced their savings raised the number of deposits **3.5×** and nearly doubled balances; a much higher interest rate did nothing ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18417)).
- Oreopoulos & Petronijevic (2018, RCT, 4,000+ university students): **one-on-one human coaching** raised grades substantially (on the order of a third of an SD); **text-message and online coaching had no effect** on grades ([JHR](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/53/2/299)). This is the single most important result for an "AI coach in a chat": a chat alone is the null arm.
- Himmler et al. (above): signed commitment + reminders worked; reminders alone did not.
- Lerner & Tetlock (1999, review): **process accountability** (justify what you did) improves effort and information use; **outcome accountability** (answer for the result) adds stress and narrows attention; accountability to an audience whose views you don't know beforehand produces the most careful work ([Psych Bulletin](https://projects.iq.harvard.edu/files/lernerlab/files/lerner_tetlock_1999.pdf)).

**Frequency and format.** Effective designs are daily during initiation (Lally's participants logged daily), weekly for maintenance (Kast; Matthews; Calzolari's weekly gym reminders). Format that fits process accountability: binary "block done / not done", one line of *evidence* (photo of the page, mock score), and the next day's plan.

**Parent, peer, tutor or coach?** Self-Determination Theory (Ryan & Deci) predicts controlling involvement undermines autonomous motivation. Pomerantz, Moorman & Litwack (2007) review: involvement helps when **autonomy-supportive, process-focused, positive in affect**, and harms when controlling and person-focused ([RER](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/003465430305567)). Vasquez et al. (2016, meta-analysis, 36 studies): parental autonomy support is positively related to achievement, autonomous motivation and psychological health, strongest when both parents provide it ([Ed Psych Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-015-9329-z)). So a parent is a good *witness* and a bad *enforcer*. A tutor is the ideal "audience with unknown views" for process reports. Peers are risky for a 16-year-old (Bursztyn & Jensen).

**Apply to Eduardo**
- Daily report goes to a human, not only to the chat. Parent receives one line at a fixed time and replies only "seen"; no commentary on quality (Pomerantz). Tutor reviews the mock log twice a week and asks *how* he practised (process), not what mark he got.
- The AI coach prepares the report and holds the record; it is not the accountability partner. Oreopoulos shows tech-only coaching is the null arm.
- Eduardo writes the rules himself; the lawyer parent formats and witnesses them. That preserves autonomy and adds seriousness.

## 3. Implementation intentions, identity, starting

- **If-then plans**: Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006, meta-analysis, 94 tests, 8,000+ participants): d = 0.65 on goal attainment ([Adv. Exp. Soc. Psych](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)). Specificity matters: Milkman et al. (2011, RCT, 3,000+ employees) — writing down **date and time** of a flu shot: +4.2 pp; date only: +1.5 pp, n.s. ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1103170108)).
- **MCII/WOOP with high-schoolers**: Duckworth et al. (2011, RCT, 66 students, mean age 16.7): MCII group completed **140 PSAT practice questions vs 84** in the placebo-writing control (+60%); standardized β = 0.27 ([Ed Psych](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/duckworth_self-regulation_11.pdf)). Wang, Wang & Gai (2021, meta-analysis, 24 effects, 15,907 participants): g = 0.34 overall; **g = 0.47 when delivered interactively by a person vs 0.28 by worksheet**; some publication bias ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/pdf)).
- **Temptation bundling**: Milkman, Minson & Volpp (2014, RCT): gym-only access to page-turner audiobooks raised visits **+51%** (full) and **+29%** (encouragement only); effect decayed over the term; 61% later paid for the device ([Mgmt Sci](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1784)).
- **Starting**: Pychyl frames procrastination as short-term mood repair; once begun, the task is perceived as less aversive (**practitioner/observational**, no RCT of a "2-minute rule" as such). The **Zeigarnik effect** (better memory for unfinished tasks) does not replicate: a 2025 meta-analysis of 37 studies excluding Zeigarnik's own finds a recall ratio of 0.99; the **Ovsiankina effect** (tendency to *resume* an interrupted task) does hold ([Nature HSSC](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w)). Masicampo & Baumeister (2011, 5 experiments): a **specific plan** for an unfinished goal eliminates its intrusive thoughts as well as finishing it ([JPSP](https://users.wfu.edu/masicaej/MasicampoBaumeister2011JPSP.pdf)).
- **Pre-registered plan sent to another person**: no single RCT tests exactly this, but it combines three effects with evidence: implementation intention (d = 0.65), task-based goal (Clark et al.), and process accountability (Lerner & Tetlock). Avoid identity-framed pledges (Gollwitzer 2009).

**Apply to Eduardo**
- Every night, one line in the chat: "Tomorrow [subject], [exact task], [start time], [place], first 2-minute action = [X]." Date+time+first action, not "study maths."
- Run WOOP once a week with the coach interactively (g = 0.47 vs 0.28 on paper): wish, best outcome, the specific obstacle he actually hits (phone, "I'll start after dinner"), if-then for it.
- Bundle a "want" (a series episode, particular music, snack) that only happens during or right after a block.

## 4. Reminder design

- Karlan, McConnell, Mullainathan & Zinman (2016, 3 RCTs): reminders raised goal attainment by **3.2 pp (+5.4%)** and balances by ~6%; reminders that **named the specific goal** worked, generic ones did not ([Mgmt Sci](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2015.2296)).
- Calzolari & Nardotto (2017, field experiment, gym): weekly reminders raised attendance up to **~9–13%**; response came **within hours** and recurred with every reminder; small carry-over after reminders stopped ([Mgmt Sci](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2499)).
- Castleman & Page (2015, RCT): personalized task-specific texts to college-intending graduates: **+3.1 pp** enrollment (+5.7 pp for the poorest) ([JEBO](https://scispace.com/papers/summer-nudging-can-personalized-text-messages-and-peer-3oxr85ibba)); Castleman later reported that scaled-up generic versions failed ([Behavioral Scientist](https://behavioralscientist.org/why-arent-text-message-interventions-designed-to-boost-college-success-working-at-scale/)).
- Null results that matter: **pure reminders did nothing** without a commitment (Himmler); **text coaching did nothing** for grades (Oreopoulos & Petronijevic).
- Health meta-analyses: SMS reminders raise appointment attendance (RR ≈ 1.09) and adherence (HIV meta-analysis SMD = 0.87) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4939231/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27477580/)); daily reminders for asthma often showed no gain, consistent with habituation.
- Fatigue: a 150-person RCT on language practice — push-notification group 82% adherence vs 67% self-scheduled vs 49% control, but a **sharp decline once reminders stopped** and fatigue with over-frequent alerts ([study](https://journals.kmanpub.com/index.php/aitechbesosci/article/view/4724)).

Design rules that fall out: (1) send **right before** the action, (2) name the **exact next action** and the commitment he already made, (3) **one** reminder per day, (4) make it require a **reply** so it becomes a report, not a broadcast, (5) escalate only on a miss, (6) plan to fade it.

**Apply to Eduardo**
- One notification, 10 minutes before the block: "19:50. You said Maths AA, Paper 1 Q1–6, desk. First action: open the paper and do Q1. Reply 'started'." Nothing else that day.
- No reply by block-end triggers the escalation (parent gets the "missed" line, stake is settled), not a second nudge. The chat never sends motivational filler.

## 5. Habit vs obligation: scaffold, then fade

- Lally et al. (2010, observational, 96 people, 84 days): median **66 days** to reach 95% of asymptotic automaticity, range **18–254**; only ~48% reached asymptote; simple behaviours were faster; **missing a single day did not derail** the curve ([EJSP](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674)).
- Kaushal & Rhodes (2015, longitudinal, 111 new gym members): **≥4 sessions/week for 6 weeks** was the minimum to establish a habit; consistency of cue and low complexity predicted it ([J Behav Med](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10865-015-9640-7)).
- Charness & Gneezy (2009, RCT): paying for 8 gym visits in a month roughly **doubled** attendance for weeks after payment stopped, driven entirely by prior non-attenders ([Econometrica](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA7416)). Royer et al.: incentive + commitment contract persisted a year. The language-app RCT above shows what happens when reminders are cut abruptly.

A 10-week "scaffold then fade" plan consistent with this:
- **Weeks 1–3 (obligation strong):** 5 blocks/week at the same time and place; nightly plan; pre-block reminder; daily evidence line to parent; stake settled daily.
- **Weeks 4–6 (habit threshold ~6 weeks at ≥4/week):** reminder only if no "started" by block time; parent report becomes an end-of-week summary; stake becomes weekly.
- **Weeks 7–10 (fade):** no pre-block reminder; self-logged streak; tutor review only; stake only if the week falls below 4 blocks. Reinstate the daily scaffold automatically if two blocks are missed in one week (Lally: one miss is fine, a pattern is not).

**Apply to Eduardo**
- Fix the cue: same start time and same desk, five days a week; the routine must be boring enough to automate.
- Tell him up front the scaffold is temporary and how it fades; it answers his "I don't want another useless thing" objection: this one is designed to disappear.

## 6. Mastery preparation: what makes a test feel trivial

- **Overlearning fades**: Rohrer & Taylor (2006, 2 experiments, 216 students): 9 vs 3 practice problems in one session had **no effect** at 1 or 4 weeks; spacing 10 problems over two sessions had **no benefit at 1 week but a very large one at 4 weeks** ([Applied Cog Psych](http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/~drohrer/pdfs/Rohrer&Taylor2006ACP.pdf)). Driskell, Willis & Copper (1992, meta-analysis, 15 studies): overlearning d ≈ 0.75 short-term, shrinking with retention interval; most studies tested within a week ([JAP](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Effect-of-overlearning-on-retention.-Driskell-Willis/78e8a299b0ba14cfb182c4f4585c207cf1d5d2af)).
- **Successive relearning to criterion**: Rawson & Dunlosky (2011, 533 students, 100,000+ scored responses): recall each concept to **3 correct retrievals**, then **relearn to 1 correct retrieval in 3 widely spaced sessions** ([JEP:General](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ934616)). In a real course, three spaced correct recalls produced **more than double** the retention of three massed correct recalls ([Ed Psych Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-013-9240-4)).
- **Mastery criterion**: Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns (1990, meta-analysis, 108 evaluations): mastery learning improves exam performance; stricter criteria (90–100%) outperform 70–80% ([RER](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543060002265)).
- **Timed practice under exam conditions**: competitive-exam coaches (JEE playbooks) converge on: one mock means nothing, **consistency across ~10 timed full-length mocks** predicts the real score, mocks taken at the real exam's clock time, and a **safe target 1–2 points above the forecast cutoff** ([Sparkl](https://sparkl.me/blog/jee/jee-main-mock-test-strategy-to-reach-the-99-percentile-a-practical-playbook/)) (**practitioner**).
- **IB grade-7 boundaries** (recent sessions, vary by session): Maths AA HL **65–75%**, AA SL 75–82%, AI HL 70–78%, AI SL 75–82% ([Tutopiya](https://www.tutopiya.com/blog/ib-dp-mathematics-grade-boundaries/)); Physics HL ~68%; Chemistry 70s; English A/History low-to-mid 80s ([RevisionDojo](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/can-you-get-a-7-on-most-ib-exams-if-you-get-80-correct-the-truth-about-ib-grade-boundaries)).

A defensible "test-readiness" definition (synthesis, not a single study): a topic is "1+1" when (a) three consecutive **cold, timed, unseen** past papers score at least **10 percentage points above the worst recent 7-boundary** for that paper, (b) every lost mark is a slip, not a concept gap, (c) he finishes with ~15% of the time unused, and (d) each concept has been retrieved correctly in **three separate spaced sessions**.

**Apply to Eduardo**
- Replace "study more of the same tonight" with spaced, mixed retrieval: each block ends with 15 minutes on material from 1–4 weeks ago.
- One cold timed past-paper section every Friday, marked honestly, logged to the tutor; readiness = the four-part threshold above, e.g., ≥80–85% cold on AA HL papers, not "I understood the notes."
- Keep a "concept ledger": each item needs three spaced correct retrievals before it is marked done.

## Recommended obligation stack for Eduardo

Ranked by evidence strength × low friction. The AI coach is the clerk and scheduler; humans hold the accountability.

1. **Task-based daily pre-registration (nightly, to the coach).** Evidence: implementation intentions d = 0.65; date+time prompts +4.2 pp; task goals (not grade goals) raise completion and grades. At **21:30** Eduardo types tomorrow's block: subject, exact task, start time, place, first 2-minute action. **If missing by 22:00**, the coach assigns a default block from the plan and tells him; the block exists regardless.

2. **Signed, witnessed soft contract (once, week 1).** Evidence: Himmler et al. (non-binding signed agreement raised exam passing; reminders alone didn't; procrastinators gained most; −9 pp dropout at 5 years). Eduardo authors the terms (5 blocks/week, 10 weeks, report format, stake, fade schedule, renegotiation clause); the lawyer parent drafts and witnesses; a copy goes to the tutor. **If broken repeatedly**, the contract's own clause reinstates the full week-1 scaffold.

3. **Daily process report to a human witness (21:00, to a parent).** Evidence: process accountability (Lerner & Tetlock); peer/witness reporting 3.5× (Kast); text-only coaching null (Oreopoulos). One line: "Block done: yes/no. Evidence: [photo/score]. Tomorrow: [task]." Parent replies "seen" and nothing else (Pomerantz: autonomy-supportive, process-focused). **If no report by 21:30**, that counts as a missed block for the stake.

4. **Immediate, loss-framed, private stake on inputs (settled daily).** Evidence: Levitt et al. (immediate > delayed; loss frame +0.18–0.25 SD; older teens respond less to trinkets); Fryer (inputs, not outputs); Giné (deposit contracts work but only for the 11% who accept them, so he must choose the stake). Eduardo deposits a weekly amount he chose (or a privilege: gaming time, weekend money); each missed block forfeits one-fifth the same evening. Never a stake on grades, never visible to classmates (Bursztyn & Jensen: 80% → 53%).

5. **One pre-block reminder that demands a reply ("started").** Evidence: specific reminders +3–5 pp (Karlan), weekly gym reminders +9–13% with response within hours (Calzolari); over-frequent alerts breed fatigue. Sent 10 minutes before the block, naming the exact first action. **If no "started" by the end of the block**, mechanism 3's miss is logged automatically; no second nudge.

6. **Twice-weekly tutor audit of the mock log (existing tutor sessions).** Evidence: successive relearning doubles retention; stricter mastery criteria outperform lax ones; coaches predict from consistency across many timed mocks. The tutor opens each session with the Friday cold-paper score, error classification (slip vs concept), and the concept ledger. **If a topic misses the readiness threshold twice**, it goes back into the spaced-relearning queue rather than "revise it again."

7. **Temptation bundle + 2-minute start (self-administered).** Evidence: +51%/+29% gym visits (Milkman), decays without the other mechanisms; starting reduces aversion (Pychyl; Ovsiankina effect replicates). One "want" reserved for study blocks; every block begins with the smallest item from the night-before plan. **If it stops working**, drop it; it is the only mechanism with no consequence attached.

Fade: weeks 1–3 all seven; weeks 4–6 drop 5 to miss-only and make 3 and 4 weekly; weeks 7–10 keep 1, 2 and 6, and self-log 3. Two misses in any week reinstates everything for the following week.
