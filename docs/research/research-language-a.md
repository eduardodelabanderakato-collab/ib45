# Language A SL (English + Portuguese): getting from 5/6 to 7 with a teacher-feedback loop

Context: IB DP1, English A SL (currently 6) and Portuguese A SL (currently 5), target 7 in both. Three HL sciences/maths compete for time. Tests announced ~2 weeks ahead. The lever the student already identified, teacher feedback before the assessed piece, is the one the research supports most strongly; the sections below turn it into numbers.

SL facts that shape everything below: Paper 1 = one guided analysis, 1 h 15 min, 35%; Paper 2 = comparative essay on two studied works, 1 h 45 min, 35%; Individual Oral = 10 min + 5 min questions, 30%. Each paper is marked on four criteria (P1: A–D at 5 marks each = 20; P2: A and B at 10, C and D at 5 = 30). Sources: [IB Language A: literature course page](https://ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/curriculum/language-and-literature/language-a-literature/), [English Collaborative P1 considerations](https://www.englishcollaborative.com/ib-english-a-literature-paper-1-assessment-considerations/).

## 1. Feedback loops: extracting the most from teacher comments

**What the evidence says**

- Feedback is among the strongest influences on achievement, but its effect is highly variable. Hattie & Timperley's synthesis of 74 meta-analyses found that the most effective feedback answers three questions: *Where am I going?* (feed up), *How am I going?* (feed back), *Where to next?* (feed forward); praise, rewards and grades alone were the least effective ([Hattie & Timperley 2007](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/003465430298487)). The 2020 re-analysis (435 studies, 61,000 participants) found an average d = 0.48, rising to d = 0.70–0.79 for *high-information* feedback, i.e. comments that explain the gap and the fix, not marks alone ([Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie 2020](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Power-of-Feedback-Revisited:-A-Meta-Analysis-of-Wisniewski-Zierer/08dba618dd4fe18935409da79873b1149d85f373)).
- Feedback only works if the student is *feedback literate*. Carless & Boud define four features: appreciating feedback, making judgments (against criteria and exemplars), managing affect (not taking a "5" personally), and taking action ([Carless & Boud 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2018.1463354)). Winstone et al. call the learner's side "proactive recipience": the student shares responsibility for making feedback work ([Winstone, Nash, Parker & Rowntree 2017](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00461520.2016.1207538)).
- *Feedback requests* (attaching specific questions to a draft) are one of the four curriculum practices recommended to build feedback literacy (Malecka, Boud & Carless 2020). The original "interactive cover sheet" study (Bloxham & Campbell 2010) found students got more useful, targeted comments, but only when their questions were specific rather than superficial ([summary in Carless & Boud](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02602938.2018.1463354)).
- Dylan Wiliam: "feedback should be more work for the recipient than the donor" and "feedback should cause thinking". The Chartered College's *Redraft and redo* guidance says redrafting works when the instruction is specific and the scope manageable (a paragraph, not the whole essay) ([Chartered College, Redraft and redo](https://my.chartered.college/research-hub/effective-feedback-redraft-and-redo/); [Feedback as Detective Work](https://newsletter.jamieleeclark.com/p/feedback-as-detective-work)). A 2025 three-draft study of lower-secondary writers found that students who treated feedback as *actionable and trustworthy* revised meaningfully between drafts, while those who did not engage barely improved ([Frontiers in Education 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1509904/full)).
- Practitioner advice for asking: state briefly what you tried to do, then ask 2–3 *specific* questions ("Does paragraph 2 evaluate effect or just name the device?") rather than "is it good?" ([UNC Writing Center, Requesting feedback](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/esl/resources/requesting-feedback-on-writing/); [Illinois Writers Workshop](https://writersworkshop.illinois.edu/resources-2/writer-resources/writing-processes/asking-for-feedback/)).

**What top Language A students do with comments** (synthesis of the above plus examiner-oriented guides): they translate every comment into criterion language, keep a running log of their own error patterns, and rewrite one paragraph to top-band standard rather than re-reading the whole essay ([RevisionDojo routine](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/preparing-for-ib-english-a-literature-exams)).

**How to apply**

- Lead time: hand the draft in **5 school days** before you need it back (teachers typically need 2–4 days; this also leaves 2 days to redraft). For this Friday's Portuguese test: essay written Monday, sent Monday night, ask for return by Wednesday, redraft Thursday.
- The request (attach to every draft, 4 lines max):
  1. Task + criteria you were targeting ("Prova 1 análise guiada, alvo: critérios A e B nível 5").
  2. One thing you deliberately tried ("tentei ligar cada recurso ao efeito no leitor").
  3. Two questions mapped to criteria ("Critério B: em quais parágrafos eu identifico sem avaliar? Critério C: o foco na pergunta-guia se perde em algum ponto?").
  4. "Uma marcação por critério (0–5) me ajuda mais que comentários gerais."
- Processing (20 min, same day feedback arrives): a **feedback log** with columns `Criterion | What I did | What the examiner wants (descriptor words) | Fix rule`. Example: `B | named "metáfora" in 3 places | "insightful and convincing analysis… evaluation of how choices shape meaning" | every device sentence ends with "which makes the reader…"`. Keep it to **1 row per comment, max 4 rows per essay**.
- Redraft cadence: **redraft within 48 h** of receiving comments, and only the paragraphs named in the log (30–40 min). Re-submit the redrafted paragraph with the log row, asking "did this fix it?" One draft → feedback → redraft cycle per week is the target rhythm; two cycles in a test fortnight.
- Before every next essay, re-read the log's *Fix rule* column (3 min). Retire a rule after it has been satisfied in 3 consecutive essays.

## 2. Weekly rhythm for a 7-level SL student (no test window)

**What practitioners recommend**

- Rotate reading/re-reading, annotation, planning and *timed* analytical writing; practise unseen prose, poetry and drama under time weekly ([RevisionDojo exam-prep routine](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/preparing-for-ib-english-a-literature-exams)). That guide's concrete model: weekdays 45–60 min (15 min flashcards on quotes/techniques, 30–40 min one timed output such as a paragraph or plan, 5 min logging one mistake pattern); weekends 90–120 min for one full timed section then rewriting one paragraph to top-band criteria.
- Read set works three times: gist, deep annotation, review ([BACC Education](https://bacceducation.com/blog/english-a-literature)). Annotate for devices, themes, character development; build a quote bank/flashcards ([Revision Village](https://www.revisionvillage.com/ib-english/literature-sl/case-studies/)).
- Paper 2: prepare **three works** so any two can be paired (1&2, 1&3, 2&3); go in with 3–4 pre-prepared pairings and "3–5 theme comparison cards" ([LitLearn P2](https://litlearn.com/ib-english-paper-2-completely-explained/); [KIS Academics](https://kisacademics.com/blog/how-to-ace-ib-english-paper-2/)).
- Realistic time budget: SL subjects 2–3 h/week outside class in a 3HL+3SL load (16–22 h/week total) ([RevisionDojo study hours](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/how-many-hours-should-i-study-per-week-in-the-ib-a-realistic-guide-by-subject-and-level)). Two Language A SL subjects therefore get **4–5 h/week combined**, which must cover reading.

**How to apply** (per language, ~2.25 h/week; alternate the "big block" between English and Portuguese)

- Reading pace: **15–20 pages/day, 5 days/week** (a 300-page novel in 3–4 weeks) or one poem/scene per session. Annotation while reading: margin code `T` theme, `Q` quotable, `X` technique, `GI` global issue candidate. Max 3 marks per page; more than that and nothing is selected.
- Quote bank: one page per work, organised by **theme → technique → 8–12 quotes of ≤15 words each**, plus one column "effect on reader" so quotes are pre-analysed. Add 3–5 quotes per week of reading; refresh 15 min/week with flashcards. Tag each quote with a possible global issue for the IO.
- Timed Paper 1: **one full 75-min guided analysis every 2 weeks** (alternating languages, so one Paper 1 per week overall), always sent to the teacher with the 4-line request. In the off week, one 25-min *plan-only* on an unseen text (thesis + 3 points + evidence).
- Paper 2: **one comparative outline (20 min) per week** on a past question: thesis, three comparison points, two quotes per work per point. Full timed Paper 2 only once per term per language until year 2.
- Feedback processing: 20 min/week (section 1).
- Daily minimum on bad weeks: 20 min reading + 5 min quote bank. Never zero, because reading debt is the one thing a 14-day ramp cannot repay.

## 3. The 14-day ramp for a Language A test

**Why it differs from maths/science**: there is no content to "cover"; the score comes from a skill (turning observations into an evaluated argument) that only improves through *write → criterion-referenced feedback → rewrite*. The last-two-weeks guidance for IB in general is "1 timed block most days, shift toward questions and marking" ([RevisionDojo, last 2 weeks](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-last-2-weeks-before-exams-what-actually-matters)); for Paper 1 the method is "format, criteria, patterns, plan, timing, feedback" ([RevisionDojo Paper 1 revision](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/step-by-step-guide-to-revising-ib-english-paper-1)), with a suggested split of 40% unseen practice, 40% set-text knowledge, 20% full mocks ([North Avenue Education](https://northaveeducation.com/blog/preparing-for-the-ib-english-exam)). Practice on IB exemplar responses "in great detail" is the examiner's own recommendation ([IBlieve examiner interview](https://iblieve.org/ib-examiner-interview-advice-for-english-a-language-and-literature/)). No single IB-teacher source publishes a day-by-day ramp; the sequence below is built from these sources and constrained by teacher turnaround time.

**How to apply** (days before test; total ≈ 7 h, i.e. the normal weekly budget doubled)

| Day | Action | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| −14 | Confirm with teacher: text type / question type, which works, which criteria. Read the four top-band descriptors aloud; copy them onto one index card. | 20 |
| −13 | Study one top-band exemplar with the criteria: mark where each descriptor phrase is satisfied. | 40 |
| −12 | **Timed essay 1** under exam conditions (75 min P1 / 105 min P2). Send same night with the 4-line request. | 75–105 |
| −11 to −9 | Off-days: reading/quote bank as usual. Self-mark essay 1 with the section 4 checklist while waiting (20 min). | 20 |
| −8/−7 | Feedback arrives (asked for by −8). Log it. **Redraft** the 1–2 weakest paragraphs; send the redraft with "did this fix it?" | 60 |
| −6 | Second exemplar or a peer's essay; mark it with the criteria (making judgments builds feedback literacy). | 30 |
| −5 | **Timed essay 2**, new text/question, applying the fix rules. Send same night. | 75–105 |
| −4 | Quote-bank drill (P2) or device-to-effect drill on 3 short unseen extracts (P1). | 30 |
| −3 | Feedback 2 arrives. Log; redraft one paragraph only. Update fix-rule card. | 40 |
| −2 | 25-min plan-only on a fresh question; compare with the fix-rule card. | 30 |
| −1 | Read your own best redrafted essay + the descriptor card. Nothing new. Sleep. | 20 |

If the teacher can only review once, keep essay 1 on −12 and make essay 2 self-marked against the log. If tests are announced with less than 14 days, compress by dropping −6 and −4, never −12 and −8/−7.

## 4. Criteria-driven writing: top-band descriptors, why 6s stay 6s, self-marking checklist

**Paper 1 (guided analysis), band 5 of 5** ([ClassLens](https://www.classlens.com/blog/ib-english-paper-1-marking-criteria-2026); [Clastify checklist](https://www.clastify.com/blog/english-a-lit-paper-1-criteria-and-checklist)):
- A Understanding and interpretation: "thorough and perceptive understanding of the literal meaning… convincing and insightful interpretation of larger implications and subtleties… references well chosen and effectively support."
- B Analysis and evaluation: "insightful and convincing analysis of textual features and/or authorial choices… very good evaluation of how such features and/or choices shape meaning." Band 4 is "appropriate and *at times* insightful"; the gap is consistency.
- C Focus and organization: "effectively organized and coherent… well focused."
- D Language: "very clear, effective, carefully chosen and precise, high degree of accuracy… register and style effective and appropriate."

**Paper 2 (comparative essay), top band** (from a teacher-prepared copy of the 2021 criteria, [acton28 PDF](https://acton28.blog/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/criteria-paper-2.pdf)):
- A (9–10): "perceptive knowledge and understanding of the works and a persuasive interpretation of their implications in relation to the question… insightful interpretation of the similarities and differences." (7–8 is "good… sustained… convincing".)
- B (9–10): "consistently insightful and convincing analysis of textual features and/or broader authorial choices… very good evaluation of how such features contribute to meaning… very good comparison and contrast of the authors' choices." (7–8: "appropriate and at times insightful… good comparison".)
- C (5): "clear and sustained focus on the task; treatment of the works is well-balanced… development logical and convincing; ideas connected in a cogent manner."
- D (5): same wording as Paper 1 D.

**Why a 6 stays a 6** (examiner and teacher sources; official subject reports live behind MyIB, so these are the public summaries):
- Evidence used to *summarise* rather than to support an interpretation; quotations from a narrow part of the text ([English Collaborative Lit P1](https://www.englishcollaborative.com/ib-english-a-literature-paper-1-assessment-considerations/)).
- Naming devices without evaluating effect; essays organised device-by-device, which "struggle to appreciate the interdependency of features' effects" (same source; [ClassLens](https://www.classlens.com/blog/ib-english-paper-1-marking-criteria-2026)).
- Analysis that stays at surface meaning ([IBlieve P1 tips](https://iblieve.org/ib-english-a-language-literature-paper-1-analysis-tips/)); rigid five-paragraph templates; answering the guiding question "diligently but descriptively".
- Ending with a "pithy summary" of what was already said (May 2023 subject-report summary, [InThinking](https://thinkib.net/englishalanglit/blog/50904/recommendations-the-may-2023-subject-report)).
- Paper 2: two separate analyses with comparison only in the conclusion; plot retelling; drifting from the question's key concept ([LitLearn](https://litlearn.com/ib-english-paper-2-completely-explained/); [ClassLens P2](https://www.classlens.com/blog/ib-english-paper-2-marking-criteria-2026); [RevisionDojo Portuguese A boundaries](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-portuguese-a-language-and-literature-hl-2025-grade-boundaries-guide)).
- Language: a 5 in D does not require flawless prose; "use the language you have" beats imported jargon (English Collaborative; IBlieve examiner).

**How to apply: self-marking checklist (run in 10 min before every submission; count YES; 10/12 = ready)**

Criterion A
1. Sentence 1–2 of the essay states a thesis that answers the guiding question / prompt in my own words.
2. Every quotation is followed by an interpretation, never by paraphrase of the plot.
3. Evidence comes from beginning, middle and end of the extract / both works equally.

Criterion B
4. Every paragraph names a choice → quotes it → states its effect on the reader/meaning ("which makes / suggests / undercuts…").
5. At least one paragraph deals with a *large* choice (structure, voice, form, argument line), not only word-level devices.
6. Paragraphs are organised by idea/claim, not by device.
7. (P2) Every body paragraph contains both works and a comparison verb (whereas, similarly, unlike).

Criterion C
8. Each topic sentence links back to the question; no paragraph could be deleted without loss.
9. The conclusion adds a "so what" (a wider implication), not a summary.

Criterion D
10. No sentence longer than ~35 words; no term I could not define.
11. Formal register throughout; 3 min proofread done.
12. Total time and length within limits (P1 SL: ~900–1100 words in 75 min; P2: ~1200–1400 in 105 min).

## 5. Portuguese A specifics

**Which course do Brazilian IB schools run?** Both exist for Portuguese (Literature is available in 55 languages, Language and Literature in 17, Portuguese included; [Wikipedia Group 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IB_Group_1_subjects)). Public school pages show *Language and Literature* is the more common choice: Colégio São Luís lists "Língua Portuguesa (Língua e Literatura)" ([São Luís IB](https://www.saoluis.org/ib/)), EAB Brasília offers "Portuguese A Language and Literature HL/SL" ([EAB](https://sites.google.com/a/eabdf.br/ib-eab/subject-groups/Group-1/portuguese-a-language-and-literature-hl-sl)), and The British School Rio offers a choice of either ([British School Rio](https://www.britishschool.g12.br/seniors)). The student should confirm which one his school runs, because Paper 1 differs: Lang & Lit gives a *non-literary* text (advert, article, infographic), Literature gives a literary extract. Grade boundaries for Portuguese A Lang & Lit HL 2025 were 85+/100 for a 7 (P1 33/40, P2 28/30, IO 34/40), i.e. the same demand as English ([RevisionDojo](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-portuguese-a-language-and-literature-hl-2025-grade-boundaries-guide)).

**ENEM habits that clash with IB criteria.** The ENEM redação is a *dissertação-argumentativa* with a thesis, external "repertório sociocultural", and a compulsory *proposta de intervenção* ([INEP guidance](https://download.inep.gov.br/publicacoes/institucionais/avaliacoes_e_exames_da_educacao_basica/textos_dissertativo_argumentativos.pdf)). Brazilian correctors themselves flag *generalização* ("todo mundo…"), *senso comum* and forced "repertório de bolso" as the most common vices ([Cria](https://blog.cria.net.br/como-argumentar-sem-generalizar-na-redacao-do-enem/); [SBT News](https://sbtnews.sbt.com.br/noticia/brasil/enem-2026-como-evitar-o-repertorio-de-bolso-na-redacao)). Transferred to IB these become exactly the 6-level faults in section 4:
- Arguing *about the topic* with outside references instead of *about the text* with quotations (Criterion A: references not "well chosen").
- Sociological generalisation in place of authorial choice → effect (Criterion B).
- Conclusion as "proposta de intervenção" or moral, instead of a "so what" about the text (Criterion C).
- Elevated, formulaic register ("É notório que…", "Diante do exposto…") replacing precise, natural language (Criterion D).
A Portuguese 5 with an English 6 is consistent with this: the same analytical skill, applied through a stronger ENEM reflex.

**Resources in Portuguese.** The IB publishes the guides only in English, French and Spanish; the Spanish *Guía de Lengua A: Literatura / Lengua y Literatura* is the closest official text and uses the same criteria ([Spanish guide PDF](https://www.cuny.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/page-assets/about/administration/offices/registrar/resources/international-baccalaureate/IB-Language-A-Literature-en-Espanol.pdf)). Unofficial Portuguese translation of the Lang & Lit guide circulates ([Scribd, Guia IB Língua e Literatura A 2021–2023](https://pt.scribd.com/document/1026729891/GUIA-IB-LI-NGUA-E-LITERATURA-A-2021-2023)). Past Portuguese A papers and a Paper 1 SL mark scheme are on Scribd ([P2 HL 2019](https://www.scribd.com/document/718286610/Portuguese-A-Language-and-literature-paper-2-HL); [P1 SL mark scheme](https://www.scribd.com/document/892648283/Portuguese-A-Language-and-Literature-Paper-1-SL-Mark-Scheme)); Clastify holds Portuguese A IO/IA exemplars ([Clastify Portuguese A Lang & Lit](https://www.clastify.com/ia/portuguese-a-lang-lit)); Spanish-language IB playlists on the IO are directly transferable ([YouTube, Lengua y Literatura IB](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLeV0C12JN8OnFN9iMdz7OKDUnpO4abuWy)). Portuguese A subject reports exist (indexed by PapaCambridge) but are behind login; ask the teacher to share the latest one.

**How to apply**
- Ask the Portuguese teacher this week: (1) Literature or Lang & Lit? (2) Can I see the last subject report and one 7-level sample? (3) Please mark my drafts *per criterion, in the IB descriptor words*, not with an ENEM-style nota.
- Run the section 4 checklist plus three Portuguese-only rules: **zero external repertório**, **zero proposta de intervenção**, **zero "é notório/diante do exposto"**. Count violations; target 0 within 3 essays.
- Translate the four criteria into a Portuguese descriptor card once (30 min) so the log and the teacher share vocabulary.
- Because Portuguese is the weaker subject, give it the full timed essay in **3 of every 4 fortnights** until it reaches consistent 16+/20 on teacher marking; English gets plan-only practice in those weeks.

## 6. Individual Oral

**What the sources say**
- The IO is 10 min prepared + 5 min questions, one literary and one non-literary text (Lang & Lit) or two literary works (Lit), through one global issue, with a 40-line extract each; the IB recommends it sit at the end of year 1 or start of year 2; teachers may guide but must not choose the issue ([RevisionDojo IO guide](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/comprehensive-guide-to-the-ib-individual-oral-io-for-language-and-literature-and-literature); [++tutors IO guide](https://www.plusplustutors.com/blog/mastering-the-ib-english-io)).
- Start focused preparation **4–6 weeks** out: weeks 1–2 choose the issue, re-read with that lens, pick extracts; week 3 detailed arguments and quotations; week 4 condense to the 10-bullet outline and rehearse aloud; weeks 5–6 practice with a teacher/partner who runs Q&A, record and review ([++tutors](https://www.plusplustutors.com/blog/mastering-the-ib-english-io)).
- A workable global issue is definable in one sentence, clearly global, narrow enough to argue, and "forces you to compare method, not just message"; "the IB IO punishes vague connections" ([RevisionDojo IO](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/comprehensive-guide-to-the-ib-individual-oral-io-for-language-and-literature-and-literature); [RevisionDojo Portuguese A](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-portuguese-a-language-and-literature-hl-2025-grade-boundaries-guide)).
- Rehearse "out loud, timed, at least 5 times", one take per session, record and review "like an examiner" (RevisionDojo IO). Extracts must be approved by the teacher at least one week before; a proposal form gets early feedback on the issue/text fit ([SaveMyExams IO](https://www.savemyexams.com/dp/english-language-and-literature/ib/english-a-language-and-literature/19/hl/revision-notes/3-individual-oral/how-to-approach-the-individual-oral/choosing-and-refining-your-global-issue-/)).
- Timing template: intro 1–1.5 min, text 1 3.5–4 min, text 2 3.5–4 min, conclusion 0.5–1 min (++tutors).

**How to apply**
- From now (DP1): every time a quote enters the quote bank, tag a candidate global issue. By the end of DP1, keep a shortlist of **3 global issues × 2 text pairings**; the IO writes itself from the bank.
- **T−6 weeks**: submit a one-page proposal (issue in one sentence, two extracts, three authorial choices per extract) and ask the teacher two questions: "Is the issue too broad?" and "Do these extracts let me compare *how*, not just *what*?"
- **T−4 weeks**: outline of 10 bullets, one page, timed read-through.
- **T−3 to T−1 weeks**: **5–8 full timed runs** (2 per week), each recorded; after each, log 2 fixes. Two of them with the teacher or a peer running 5 min of questions; ask the teacher to mark against the four IO criteria.
- **T−7 days**: extracts approved, final outline frozen; only delivery changes after this.
- Per-run cost: 15 min speaking + 15 min review = 30 min; total IO preparation ≈ 8–10 h spread over 6 weeks, which fits inside the 2.25 h/week budget if the timed-essay slot is swapped for IO runs in those weeks.

### Summary of the numbers
- Draft to teacher **5 school days** before you need it; 4-line request with 2 criterion-mapped questions; log ≤4 rows; redraft the named paragraphs within **48 h**.
- Off-window week per language: **~2.25 h** = 15–20 pages/day reading, 1 timed Paper 1 per fortnight (or plan-only), 1 Paper 2 outline, 20 min feedback processing.
- Test ramp: essays on **−12** and **−5**, feedback by **−8** and **−3**, redrafts on **−7** and **−3**, nothing new on **−1**; ≈7 h total.
- Self-mark: 12-item checklist, submit at **10/12**; Portuguese adds 3 anti-ENEM rules at 0 violations.
- IO: proposal **6 weeks** out, **5–8 recorded timed runs**, 2 with teacher Q&A, extracts approved **7 days** before.
