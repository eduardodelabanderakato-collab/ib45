# IB 45 Research Brief: Syllabus Maps, Grade-7 Thresholds, Resources, IA/EE Timeline

Prepared 2026-09-22 for a DP1 student (started Aug 2026, exams May 2028, Brazil). Subjects: Math AA HL, Physics HL, Chemistry SL, Economics HL, Portuguese A SL, English A SL. Current position: Math just finished exponentials/logs; Physics in A.2 (forces/momentum); Econ starting 2.5 elasticities.

Caveat on boundaries: the IB does not publish grade boundaries publicly; the figures below are from third-party compilations of coordinator-only data and shift a few percent each session ([RevisionDojo](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/what-are-the-assessment-criteria-for-ib-english-a-language-and-literature-oral-and-written-exams), [GradePod](https://gradepod.com/blog/ib-physics-grade-boundaries/)). Treat them as calibration targets, not promises.

## 1. Syllabus maps and predicted "what comes next"

### 1a. Mathematics: Analysis & Approaches HL (2019 syllabus, first exams 2021)

Five topics, 240 hours at HL: Number & Algebra (29 h), Functions (32 h), Geometry & Trigonometry (51 h), Statistics & Probability (33 h), Calculus (55 h) ([Pietro Meloni](https://www.pietromeloni.com/insights/ib-math-aa-hl-syllabus-complete-guide)). Papers: P1 (no GDC, 110 marks, 2 h, 30%), P2 (GDC, 110 marks, 2 h, 30%), P3 (GDC, 55 marks, 1 h, 20%), IA exploration (20 marks, 20%).

Full subtopic list (official numbering):
- **1 Number & Algebra:** 1.1 scientific notation; 1.2 arithmetic sequences; 1.3 geometric sequences; 1.4 financial applications; 1.5 exponents & logs; 1.6 simple proof; 1.7 (AHL) log laws / rational exponents; 1.8 (AHL) sum of infinite geometric series; 1.9 binomial theorem; 1.10 (AHL) counting, binomial with fractional/negative exponents; 1.11 (AHL) partial fractions; 1.12 (AHL) complex numbers, Cartesian; 1.13 (AHL) modulus-argument, Euler form; 1.14 (AHL) complex roots, De Moivre; 1.15 (AHL) proof by induction, contradiction, counterexample; 1.16 (AHL) systems of linear equations.
- **2 Functions:** 2.1 lines; 2.2 functions/domain/range/inverse; 2.3 graphs; 2.4 key features; 2.5 composite/inverse; 2.6 quadratics; 2.7 discriminant; 2.8 reciprocal & rational; 2.9 exponential & log functions; 2.10 solving equations; 2.11 transformations; 2.12 (AHL) polynomials, factor/remainder theorems, sum/product of roots; 2.13 (AHL) rational functions; 2.14 (AHL) odd/even, inverse, self-inverse; 2.15 (AHL) solving inequalities; 2.16 (AHL) modulus functions/equations.
- **3 Geometry & Trig:** 3.1 3D distance/midpoint/volumes; 3.2 sine/cosine rule; 3.3 applications (bearings, elevation); 3.4 radians, arcs, sectors; 3.5 unit circle, exact values; 3.6 identities; 3.7 trig functions & graphs; 3.8 solving trig equations; 3.9 (AHL) reciprocal trig, inverse trig; 3.10 (AHL) compound & double angle; 3.11 (AHL) more identities/symmetry; 3.12–3.18 (AHL) vectors: concepts, scalar product, lines, intersections, cross product, planes, line/plane intersections.
- **4 Statistics & Probability:** 4.1–4.4 data, presentation, central tendency/spread, correlation/regression; 4.5–4.6 probability & Venn/tree; 4.7 discrete random variables; 4.8 binomial; 4.9 normal; 4.10 regression of x on y (etc.); 4.11 conditional probability; 4.12 (AHL) standardisation; 4.13 (AHL) Bayes; 4.14 (AHL) continuous random variables, pdf, mode/median/variance.
- **5 Calculus:** 5.1 limits/derivative; 5.2 increasing/decreasing; 5.3 derivative of polynomials; 5.4 tangents/normals; 5.5 integration intro; 5.6 chain/product/quotient rule; 5.7 second derivative; 5.8 optimisation, points of inflexion; 5.9 kinematics; 5.10 integration by substitution; 5.11 definite integrals, areas; 5.12 (AHL) continuity, first principles; 5.13 (AHL) L'Hôpital; 5.14 (AHL) implicit, related rates, optimisation; 5.15 (AHL) more derivatives (sec, csc, arcsin…); 5.16 (AHL) integration by parts, partial fractions; 5.17 (AHL) areas/volumes of revolution; 5.18 (AHL) differential equations, Euler, separable, integrating factor; 5.19 (AHL) Maclaurin series.

**Typical DP1 teaching order.** Christos Nikolaidis' published suggested order (a Year 1 = ~60% / Year 2 = ~40% split) for a combined SL/HL class is: Number & Algebra (rounding, sequences, series, geometric applications, binomial theorem, proof, induction, systems of equations) → Functions: lines, quadratics → complex numbers (Cartesian only) → the rest of Functions (polynomials, sum/product of roots, domain/range, composition, inverse, transformations, asymptotes, rational functions & partial fractions, inequalities, **exponents/exponential functions, logarithms, exponential equations**, modulus) → Trigonometry (3D geometry, sine/cosine rule, unit circle, identities, trig functions/equations, inverse trig) → complex numbers polar part (De Moivre, roots of z^n = a) → start of Calculus (limits, derivatives, tangents/normals, chain rule and the rest of differentiation). Year 2 = Statistics & Probability, Vectors (3.10–3.18), and the remaining Calculus (integration techniques, volumes, differential equations, Maclaurin) ([Nikolaidis, "Suggested teaching order for Math AA SL and HL" PDF](https://0e5b113685.clvaw-cdnwnd.com/41af4b1467c93b79b2d59d3f8552dcc1/200000609-c4ab5c4ab8/MAA%20SL-HL%20teaching%20order.pdf?ph=0e5b113685)). Pietro Meloni's guide says the same thing in coarser terms: Year 1 = Topics 1, 2, 3 "including complex numbers (Euler form), proof by induction, function graphing, and the fundamentals of vector geometry"; Year 2 = advanced calculus, differential equations, Maclaurin.

**Prediction for what comes next after exp/logs:** most likely **modulus functions/equations (2.16) → Trigonometry (3.1–3.11: sine/cosine rule, radians, unit circle, identities, trig functions, trig equations, compound/double angle)**, then **complex numbers in polar/Euler form and De Moivre (1.13–1.14)**, then **differentiation (5.1–5.8)**. Pre-learn trigonometry now; it is the single largest topic (51 h) and it feeds directly into complex numbers, vectors and calculus.

### 1b. Physics HL (2023 syllabus, first exams 2025)

Five themes, 24 topics; SL 150 h, HL 240 h. Papers ([GradePod](https://www.gradepod.com/blog/new-ib-physics-syllabus-2023-first-exams-2025), [Clastify](https://www.clastify.com/blog/ib-physics-paper-1)): Paper 1 = 1A (40 MCQ at HL, 30 at SL) + 1B (data-based questions), HL 2 h, 36%; Paper 2 short/extended response, HL 90 marks, 2 h 30, 44%; IA (Scientific Investigation) 24 marks, 20%. Calculator and data booklet allowed on both papers.

| Theme | Topics (HL-only in bold) |
|---|---|
| A Space, time & motion (27 h SL / 42 h HL) | A.1 Kinematics; A.2 Forces & momentum; A.3 Work, energy & power; **A.4 Rigid body mechanics**; **A.5 Galilean & special relativity** |
| B Particulate nature of matter (24/32) | B.1 Thermal energy transfers; B.2 Greenhouse effect; B.3 Gas laws; **B.4 Thermodynamics**; B.5 Current & circuits |
| C Wave behaviour (17/29) | C.1 Simple harmonic motion (HL ext.); C.2 Wave model; C.3 Wave phenomena (HL ext.); C.4 Standing waves & resonance; C.5 Doppler effect (HL ext.) |
| D Fields (19/38) | D.1 Gravitational fields (HL ext.); D.2 Electric & magnetic fields (HL ext.); D.3 Motion in EM fields; **D.4 Induction** |
| E Nuclear & quantum (23/39) | E.1 Structure of the atom (HL ext.); **E.2 Quantum physics**; E.3 Radioactive decay (HL ext.); E.4 Fission; E.5 Fusion & stars |

Plus the cross-cutting "Tools" (experimental techniques, technology, mathematics: sig figs, uncertainties, graphing) examined mainly in Paper 1B.

**Typical DP1 order.** A published Year-1 schedule for the 2025 course runs: measurement & uncertainty → A.1 Kinematics → A.2 Forces & momentum (Newton's laws, equilibrium, friction, terminal velocity, impulse, momentum conservation, 2D collisions, circular motion) → A.3 Work, energy & power → A.4 Rigid body mechanics (HL) → B.1 Thermal energy transfer → B.2 Greenhouse effect → B.3 Gas laws → C.1 SHM at end of Year 1; Year 2 = C.2–C.5 waves, D fields, E nuclear/quantum, plus remaining HL content (B.4, D.4, E.2, A.5) ([IBDP Physics Year 1 schedule](https://sites.google.com/view/ibdpphysicsyear1/schedule-materials)). Theme A is the largest allocation, so most schools spend the whole first semester on it ([myrevisionagent](https://myrevisionagent.com/guides/ib-physics)).

**Prediction after A.2 momentum:** **circular motion (end of A.2) → A.3 Work, energy, power → A.4 Rigid body mechanics (HL: torque, moment of inertia, angular momentum, rolling)**, then Theme B thermal (B.1 → B.2 → B.3 → B.4 HL) and B.5 circuits. Pre-learn energy/power now and rotational mechanics right after; A.4 is new to the 2025 syllabus and has few practice questions, so it separates HL candidates.

### 1c. Chemistry SL (2023 syllabus, first exams 2025)

Two themes, Structure and Reactivity, "Structure determines reactivity, which in turn transforms structure". SL 110 h content + 40 h experimental programme (including 10 h collaborative sciences project and 10 h scientific investigation). Papers: P1 = 1A (30 MCQ at SL) + 1B (data-based, 25 marks at SL), 1 h 30, 36%; P2 = 50 marks at SL, 1 h 30, 44%; IA 20% ([PrepScholar](https://blog.prepscholar.com/ib-chemistry-syllabus-hl-sl), [Clastify P1](https://www.clastify.com/blog/ib-chemistry-paper-1)).

| Structure | Reactivity |
|---|---|
| S1.1 Introduction to the particulate nature of matter; S1.2 The nuclear atom; S1.3 Electron configurations; S1.4 Counting particles by mass: the mole; S1.5 Ideal gases | R1.1 Measuring enthalpy changes; R1.2 Energy cycles in reactions; R1.3 Energy from fuels; R1.4 Entropy & spontaneity (HL) |
| S2.1 The ionic model; S2.2 The covalent model; S2.3 The metallic model; S2.4 From models to materials | R2.1 How much? amount of chemical change; R2.2 How fast? rate; R2.3 How far? equilibrium |
| S3.1 The periodic table: classification of elements; S3.2 Functional groups: classification of organic compounds | R3.1 Proton transfer (acids/bases); R3.2 Electron transfer (redox); R3.3 Electron sharing (radicals); R3.4 Electron-pair sharing (nucleophiles/electrophiles) |

The IB explicitly says there is no prescribed order and that Structure and Reactivity should be taught in parallel ([Pearson](https://www.pearson.com/international-schools/international-schools-blog/2023/03/changes-to-the-ib-dp-chemistry-subject-guide.html), [IBlieve](https://iblieve.org/the-chemistry-behind-the-ib-chemistry-syllabus-update/)). In practice most schools keep the old topic order. A published 2025 course outline (Surrey Schools, HL) maps it as: **Year 1** = measurement & data processing (Tools) → stoichiometry (S1.4, S1.5, R2.1) → atomic structure (S1.1–S1.3) → periodicity (S3.1) → bonding & structure (S2.1–S2.4) → energetics (R1.1–R1.3); **Year 2** = kinetics (R2.2) → equilibrium (R2.3) → acids & bases (R3.1) → redox (R3.2) → organic chemistry (S3.2, R3.3, R3.4) → measurement & analysis (spectroscopy in S3.2/Tools) ([Surrey Schools course outline PDF](https://media.surreyschools.ca/media/Default/medialib/2025-ib-chemistry-course-outline.9b1356195057.pdf)). Use that as the DP1 pre-learn order for Chem SL, in the sequence S1.1 → S1.2 → S1.3 → S1.4 → S1.5 → R2.1 → S3.1 → S2.1 → S2.2 → S2.3 → S2.4 → R1.1 → R1.2 → R1.3.

### 1d. Economics HL (2020 syllabus, first exams 2022)

Four units; HL 240 h (Unit 1: 10 h; Unit 2 micro: 70 h HL; Unit 3 macro: 75 h; Unit 4 global: 65 h) ([PrepScholar](https://blog.prepscholar.com/the-complete-ib-economics-syllabus-sl-and-hl)). Nine key concepts thread through everything: scarcity, choice, efficiency, equity, economic well-being, sustainability, change, interdependence, intervention ([Clastify](https://www.clastify.com/knowledge-hub/post/ib-economics-syllabus-topics)). HL assessment: P1 extended response 25 marks, 1 h 15, 20%; P2 data response 40 marks, 1 h 45, 30%; P3 (HL only) policy paper, two 30-mark questions, 60 marks, 1 h 45, 30%; IA portfolio of three commentaries 45 marks, 20% ([econlearn](https://www.econlearn.org/ib-economics/paper-1), [RevisionDojo Econ HL TZ1 2025](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-economics-hl-grade-boundaries-2025-complete-breakdown-timezone-1)).

- **Unit 1:** 1.1 What is economics? 1.2 How do economists approach the world?
- **Unit 2 Micro:** 2.1 Demand; 2.2 Supply; 2.3 Competitive market equilibrium; 2.4 Critique of maximising behaviour (behavioural econ); **2.5 Elasticity of demand (PED, YED)**; 2.6 Elasticity of supply (PES, incl. HL primary commodities); 2.7 Role of government (indirect taxes, subsidies, price controls, HL nudges); 2.8 Market failure: externalities & common pool resources; 2.9 Public goods; 2.10 Asymmetric information (HL); 2.11 Market power (HL: monopoly, oligopoly, monopolistic competition, perfect competition); 2.12 Inability to achieve equity (HL).
- **Unit 3 Macro:** 3.1 Measuring economic activity; 3.2 AD/AS; 3.3 Macro objectives (growth, unemployment, inflation, equity); 3.4 Inequality & poverty; 3.5 Monetary policy; 3.6 Fiscal policy; 3.7 Supply-side policies.
- **Unit 4 Global:** 4.1 Benefits of trade; 4.2 Types of protection; 4.3 Arguments for/against protection; 4.4 Economic integration; 4.5 Exchange rates; 4.6 Balance of payments; 4.7 Sustainable development; 4.8 Measuring development; 4.9 Barriers to growth/development; 4.10 Strategies.

Schools almost universally teach the units in numerical order (micro in DP1 semester 1–2, macro in DP1 semester 2 into DP2, global economy in DP2) ([thinkib](https://thinkib.net/economics/page/20132/units-1-2-microeconomics), [Dachpian](https://dachpian.weebly.com/unit-25--26---microeconomics-elasticity.html)). **Prediction after 2.5:** **2.6 PES → 2.7 government intervention (indirect tax/subsidy/price ceiling & floor diagrams plus the HL calculations of revenue, expenditure, welfare loss) → 2.8 externalities → 2.9 public goods → 2.10–2.12 HL market failures and theory of the firm (market structures)**. Elasticities and 2.7 calculations are the backbone of Paper 3 quantitative questions, so drill them now.

### 1e. Language A (English A SL and Portuguese A SL; 2019 syllabus, first exams 2021)

Both Language A: Literature and Language A: Language & Literature share the same architecture: three areas of exploration (Readers, writers and texts; Time and space; Intertextuality) and seven concepts (identity, culture, creativity, communication, perspective, transformation, representation) ([Clastify Lit](https://www.clastify.com/knowledge-hub/post/ib-language-a-literature-syllabus-topics), [IBO](https://ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/curriculum/language-and-literature/language-a-literature/)). Literature SL studies a minimum of 9 works (Clastify's page lists 7; the guide itself says 9 at SL, 13 at HL); Lang & Lit SL studies 4 literary works plus non-literary bodies of work. SL assessment: Paper 1 guided analysis (Lit: one of two unseen passages with a guiding question, 20 marks, 1 h 15, 35%; Lang & Lit: one of two unseen non-literary texts, 20 marks, 35%); Paper 2 comparative essay on two studied works (30 marks, 1 h 45, 35%); Individual Oral (10 min + 5 min questions, 40 marks, 30%) ([ibcalculator](https://www.ibcalculator.com/subjects/literature), [RevisionDojo criteria](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/what-are-the-assessment-criteria-for-ib-english-a-language-and-literature-oral-and-written-exams)). There is no fixed content order; schools sequence by the works they choose. Confirm with each teacher which works are earmarked for the IO (usually DP1 works) vs Paper 2 (usually DP2 works).

## 3. What distinguishes a 7

### Math AA HL
- Marks are M (method), A (accuracy), R (reasoning) with positive marking and follow-through; "method marks survive a wrong final answer", so write the formula and substitution before computing ([mathzem](https://mathzem.com/ib-maths-aa-hl-exam-technique/), [plusplustutors](https://www.plusplustutors.com/blog/ib-maths-aa-hl-paper-3-strategies-that-actually-work)).
- "Show that": work forwards from the given, every algebraic step; do not work backwards. "Hence": you must use the previous part. Give exact answers on P1; on P2 give 3 s.f. unless told otherwise and keep full precision in the GDC.
- Paper 3 "rewards mathematical communication more than any other component": define variables, state assumptions, say what you are doing. The first marks of Q2 are easier than the last marks of Q1, so move on ([RevisionDojo P3](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/math-hl-paper-3)).
- RevisionDojo's 7-band description: "fewer unforced errors in algebraic manipulation, clear working that makes method marks easy to award, better tactical decisions under time".

### Physics HL
- Command terms drive the mark scheme: State (short), Define (precise, with equation), Calculate (formula, substitution, answer with unit and correct s.f.), Derive (every step from established equations), Explain (physical mechanism, not restatement), Sketch vs Draw (labelled axes/shape vs accurate labelled), Deduce, Suggest, Estimate, Compare, Evaluate ([Tutopiya command terms](https://www.tutopiya.com/blog/ib/command-words-keywords/ib-dp-physics-command-terms-keywords/)).
- Uncertainties: absolute ± half smallest division (analogue), half-range for repeats, quote to 1 s.f., add absolute for ±, add percentage for ×/÷, multiply percentage by the power; these are hit hardest in Paper 1B ([SaveMyExams](https://www.savemyexams.com/dp/physics/ib/23/hl/revision-notes/tools/tool-3-mathematics/processing-uncertainties/), [IB Demystified](https://ibdemystified.com/ib-physics-hl-handling-uncertainties-notes/)).
- Know the data booklet cold (equation locations and symbol meanings) so P1A MCQs take < 3 min each. Paper 2 "rewards structure: state the principle, write the equation, substitute carefully" ([RevisionDojo Physics HL](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-physics-hl-grade-boundaries-2025-complete-breakdown-timezone-0)).
- Answers must carry SI units and sensible s.f. (match the data given); a bare number loses the A mark.

### Chemistry SL
- Paper 1B (25 marks SL) is the differentiator: experimental technique, data processing, evaluating method. "Success here comes from fluent use of the Chemistry Data Booklet, comfort with error and uncertainty, and a calm, methodical writing style" ([Learnmate](https://learnmate.com.au/ib-chemistry-paper-1-guide-2025-paper-1a-1b/), [tutorsplus](https://tutorsplus.com/ib-chemistry-paper-1a-1b-the-key-to-passing-your-exam/)).
- Make core practicals automatic: standard solutions/titration, calorimetry, rates experiments; know how to interpret graphs and spot anomalies.
- Mark-scheme habits: state symbols in equations when asked, balanced equations, units on ΔH (kJ mol⁻¹), sign conventions, s.f. matching data, ECF (error carried forward) only awarded if working is shown. Structure calculations with labelled steps so partial credit is possible.
- Use the data booklet sections actively (bond enthalpies, electronegativities, Ka/pKa, standard electrode potentials, IR/NMR tables).

### Economics HL
- **Paper 1 (25 marks, 1 of 3 questions).** Part (a) 10 marks: define terms, one accurate fully labelled diagram with arrows showing the change, explain the mechanism step by step referring to the diagram; ~25 min. Part (b) 15 marks: definitions, diagram if useful, 2–3 analytical points applied to a specific real-world example, evaluation woven throughout (CLASPP: conclusions, long-run vs short-run, assumptions, stakeholders, priorities, pros/cons), and a justified conclusion; ~45–50 min. Top band requires "a specific real-world example plus real evaluation with a justified conclusion; 'it depends' with no decision caps you in the middle band" ([econlearn](https://www.econlearn.org/ib-economics/paper-1), [KIS Academics](https://kisacademics.com/blog/how-to-perfect-your-ib-economics-long-answer-responses-paper-1/)). Build a bank of real-world examples organised by syllabus section with four facts each (what, when, a number, the mechanism).
- **Paper 2 (40 marks, data response).** Around 8 short questions (1–4 marks) plus a 15-mark question. Markers cap answers that ignore the extract even if theoretically correct; "actually use the numbers, trends, and extracts" ([tutorsplus](https://tutorsplus.com/how-to-nail-your-ib-economics-paper-2-practical-tips/), [RevisionDojo Econ HL](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-economics-hl-grade-boundaries-2025-complete-breakdown-timezone-1)). 4 marks = 4 minutes.
- **Paper 3 (HL, 2 × 30 marks).** Part (a) 20 marks of calculation/interpretation (elasticities, tax revenue, welfare loss, multipliers, GDP, exchange-rate maths); part (b) 10 marks policy recommendation. "Show all working... then explain what the number means economically"; method marks rescue wrong final values ([IBonomics P3](https://www.ibonomics.org/exam-information/paper-3), [theeconomicstutor](https://www.theeconomicstutor.com/ib-economics-hl-paper-3-guide/)).
- **IA.** Three 800-word commentaries, one per unit (micro, macro, global), each on a different key concept and a different recent news source, at least one well-labelled diagram each, diagrams/labels outside the word count; criterion E (evaluation) needs room, so run close to 800 ([Lanterna IA guide](https://lanterna.com/resources/ib-economics-ia-guide-2026), [IA Studio](https://iastudio.co.uk/economics-ia-guide)).

### Language A SL (English and Portuguese)
- All three components use Criteria A–D (10 marks each on IO; scaled on papers): A knowledge/understanding/interpretation, B analysis & evaluation, C focus/organisation/development, D language ([RevisionDojo criteria](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/what-are-the-assessment-criteria-for-ib-english-a-language-and-literature-oral-and-written-exams)).
- **IO (40 marks, 30%).** 10 minutes on one literary and one non-literary (Lang & Lit) or two literary (Lit) extracts linked through a global issue, then 5 min questions. Top band: "a specific, sustained argument rather than loose observations", references to both extract and wider work only where they strengthen the argument, choice → effect → purpose chains; the global issue must be explicit and drive selection ([RevisionDojo](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/what-are-the-assessment-criteria-for-ib-english-a-language-and-literature-oral-and-written-exams), [English Collaborative](https://www.englishcollaborative.com/ib-english-a-literature-individual-oral-assessment-considerations/)). 34/40 is the 7 line.
- **Paper 1 (20 marks).** Fixed method: thesis + 3 technique-driven paragraphs each linking a choice to effect and purpose, answering the guiding question. "Summary is the floor; interpretation is the staircase". 16–17/20 is the 7 line.
- **Paper 2 (30 marks).** Comparative essay on two studied works: both texts in every paragraph, not two mini-essays; plan and choose the prompt carefully before writing. 26/30 is the 7 line ([RevisionDojo Lit SL](https://www.revisiondojo.com/blog/ib-english-a-literature-sl-grade-boundaries-2025-tz2-orals-papers-and-final-grades)).
- Portuguese A applies identical criteria; the RevisionDojo Portuguese guide stresses rehearsing the IO aloud until the global-issue link is explicit and "using rubric language during all practice work".

## 4. Best resources per subject (ranked for pre-learning, then for targeted practice)

General rule of thumb from student rankings: Revision Village for maths/sciences question banks, Christos Nikolaidis for AA notes, Grade Gorilla for free physics/chem quizzes, EconplusDal for economics video, IB Docs/IB Documents for past papers ([TSR ranking thread](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=5324454), [Hack Your Course](https://www.hackyourcourse.com/ib-free-resources/)).

**Math AA HL**
1. [Christos Nikolaidis](https://www.christosnikolaidis.com/en/) (free): lecture notes, exercises with solutions, HL P3 questions and tests, organised by the same 1.1–5.19 numbering; the best pre-learning resource because each section is a self-contained lesson.
2. [Revision Village](https://www.revisionvillage.com/ib-math/analysis-and-approaches-hl/) (freemium; paid ~US$100+/yr): topic question bank graded easy/medium/hard, video solutions, past-paper walkthroughs, mock exams incl. P3. Best for targeted practice and P3.
3. Haese/Oxford textbooks you already have: use for worked examples; do the "hard" exercises only.
4. SaveMyExams AA HL (paid): topic-tagged exam questions with mark schemes.
5. Past papers + mark schemes (IBDocs / school): from DP2 onward, timed.

**Physics HL**
1. Kognity (school licence): read the section before class, do the section quizzes.
2. [Grade Gorilla](https://www.gradegorilla.com/IB-physics-revision-questions.php) (free): self-marking topic quizzes; fastest check that pre-learning stuck.
3. Revision Village Physics HL (paid): 2025-syllabus question bank and past papers with videos.
4. [SaveMyExams IB Physics 2025](https://www.savemyexams.com/dp/physics/ib/23/hl/revision-notes/) (paid, partial free tier): examiner-written notes are the best for reading ahead of class and for Tools/uncertainties.
5. [GradePod](https://gradepod.com/blog/ib-physics-syllabus/) (free blog, paid course): syllabus objective lists and exam technique.
6. Physics & Maths Tutor: A-level only, no IB section, so use only for topic-adjacent practice (mechanics, waves) ([PMT/SME comparison](https://cognito.org/blog/physics-and-maths-tutor-vs-save-my-exams)).

**Chemistry SL**
1. Kognity for reading ahead; [ibchem.com](https://ibchem.com/IB25/index-sl.php) (free) for 2025-syllabus notes by S/R code.
2. Grade Gorilla chemistry quizzes (free).
3. Revision Village Chemistry SL question bank (paid).
4. SaveMyExams IB Chemistry (paid) for Paper 1B style data questions.
5. Official IB Chemistry data booklet: print it and annotate.

**Economics HL**
1. [EconplusDal](https://www.youtube.com/@EconplusDal) (free YouTube): the standard for diagrams and micro/macro theory; watch the video for the next sub-topic before class.
2. Kognity + Tragakes/Pearson textbook for definitions and the exact syllabus wording.
3. [IBonomics](https://www.ibonomics.org/exam-information/paper-1) and [econlearn](https://www.econlearn.org/ib-economics/paper-1) (free): paper-specific technique, P3 formula lists.
4. Revision Village Economics (paid) and SaveMyExams Economics (paid): topic question banks with model answers; "The IB Economist" for real-world example banks.
5. Past papers 2022+ only (new syllabus) for P3.

**Language A (English and Portuguese)**
1. Your set works and class annotations first; no substitute.
2. [Lanterna IO guide](https://lanterna.com/resources/master-the-ib-english-io) and RevisionDojo criteria articles (free) for rubric language.
3. Philpot Education / thinkib English A pages (school licence) for Paper 1 text types and model IOs.
4. For Portuguese A: past IB Portuguese A papers via the school coordinator; there are few third-party resources, so the teacher's exemplars matter most.

**Cross-subject**
- IB Documents / IBDocs (free): past papers, mark schemes, subject reports. Read the subject reports; they state exactly where candidates lost marks.
- Clastify (free/paid): exemplar IAs and EEs with grades.
- Official IB subject guides (from the coordinator): the only authoritative topic lists; keep a copy of each.

## 5. Internal assessments, TOK and EE timeline (typical, to be confirmed against the school's calendar)

Key fixed dates for a May 2028 candidate, extrapolating from May 2027 published dates: TOK prescribed titles released start of September of DP2 (Sept 2027); school uploads EE and TOK essay to IB by ~15 March 2028; written exams late April to late May 2028 ([Notesmakr](https://notesmakr.com/blog/how-to-study-for-ib-exams), [IBO EE deadline](https://www.ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/curriculum/dp-core/extended-essay/submission-deadline/)).

| Component | Typical timing | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TOK exhibition | Designed for DP1; most schools run it in DP1 semester 2 (Feb–June 2027), some early DP2 | Three objects + commentary, 950 words ([thinkib TOK](https://thinkib.net/tok/page/56550/what-is-a-tok-exhibition), [Lanterna timeline](https://lanterna.com/resources/ib-diploma-timeline)) |
| Economics IA (3 commentaries) | Commentary 1 (micro) DP1 term 2 (~Feb–Apr 2027); commentary 2 (macro) DP1 term 3 or early DP2; commentary 3 (global) DP2 term 1–2 | Only IA that is a portfolio; each on an article < 1 year old at submission ([Tutopiya Econ IA](https://www.tutopiya.com/blog/ib-economics-ia-portfolio-criteria-deep-dive/)) |
| Extended Essay | Subject/supervisor choice Nov–Mar of DP1; research plan finalised by end of DP1; write over the DP1→DP2 summer (Jul–Aug 2027); first full draft Sept–Oct 2027; final Dec 2027–Feb 2028 | Consider Economics or Physics EE to double-dip subject knowledge ([Lanterna](https://lanterna.com/resources/ib-diploma-timeline), [Sparkl master timeline](https://sparkl.me/blog/ib/ib-dp-ia-ee-tok-the-master-timeline-to-prevent-deadline-collisions/)) |
| Math IA (exploration) | Topic chosen end of DP1 or over summer 2027; 3–4 month cycle (topic → investigation → draft → feedback → final); first-wave IA submission Sept–Oct 2027 | Math is typically the first IA due in DP2 ([Tutopiya Math IA](https://www.tutopiya.com/blog/ib-mathematics-ia-exploration-criteria-deep-dive/)) |
| Physics IA / Chemistry IA | Group 4 collaborative project end of DP1 (Apr–Jun 2027); individual scientific investigation data collection Sept–Nov 2027, write-up Nov 2027–Jan 2028 | 10 h allocated in the experimental programme; sciences usually due Nov–Jan ([Lanterna](https://lanterna.com/resources/ib-diploma-timeline)) |
| Language A IO (English and Portuguese) | Scheduled by the teacher, usually DP2 between Nov 2027 and Feb 2028; some schools run it end of DP1 | Two orals (one per language) so stagger preparation ([Lanterna IO](https://lanterna.com/resources/ace-your-ib-language-oral-ab-guide)) |
| TOK essay | Titles Sept 2027; drafts Oct–Dec 2027; final Jan–Feb 2028; upload by mid-March 2028 | 1,600 words, one interaction with supervisor form |
| Mocks | Mid-year DP1 exams (Dec 2026 or Mar 2027), end-DP1 exams (Jun 2027), DP2 mocks (Nov 2027–Jan 2028) | Predicted grades for university applications come from DP2 mocks |

**Implication for the study plan.** The dangerous window is September 2027 to February 2028 when the EE draft, Math IA, science IAs, Econ commentary 3, two IOs and the TOK essay all land while new content (Math Year-2 calculus/vectors, Physics Themes D–E, Chem R3/organic, Econ Unit 4) is still being taught ([Sparkl](https://sparkl.me/blog/ib/ib-dp-ia-ee-tok-the-master-timeline-to-prevent-deadline-collisions/)). The pre-learning plan should therefore aim to be one full unit ahead of class by June 2027 in Math and Physics, so DP2 semester 1 can absorb the IA/EE load, and the summer of 2027 should be reserved for the EE first draft and the Math IA investigation.
